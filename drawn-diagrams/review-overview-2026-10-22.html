Drawn diagrams

When a slide needs something drawn (a heart, a plant cell, the ear, a cycling route, a free throw), Gamma’s AI draws it in the deck’s theme colors, and every label stays editable text. Four PRs, stacked. This page is the overview for reviewers.

How it works

  1. The writer writes <diagram illustrate="cross-section of the human heart"> with up to 8 short labels.
  2. The agent notices the diagram needs a drawing and calls the server.
  3. The server asks the AI to draw it using a color key: a few tone colors, plus one unique color per label for an empty label box and a pointer line. No text in the picture.
  4. Code, not a model, reads the drawing back: each color becomes a theme class, and each label box becomes a text box. If a label is missing, it tries once more. Users are only charged for a drawing that worked.
  5. The editor paints the drawing with the theme and builds an editable text box for every label.

What we tested

  • End to end: a real agent session wrote a heart card, Quiver drew it in 47s for $0.17, all 6 labels found, charged once.
  • The reader is checked against 8 real drawings, one for each quirk it handles.
  • The writer: over 23 test briefs, drawn diagrams showed up on every physical subject (ear, heart, plant cell, free throw, brain, cycling route), and never on charts, scales or business decks.
  • Two supported models: Quiver drawing directly (every label found in 10 of 10) and, as a backup, GPT Image painting it and Quiver converting it to SVG (9 of 10). Switching between them is a flag change. Gemini (2 of 6) and Ideogram’s new SVG model (0 of 11) didn’t follow the colors, so they aren’t supported.

Before turning it on

  • Flags (all off by default): diagramDrawing, quiverArrow2, and the writer’s harnessDrawnDiagrams, which only takes effect when the other two are on.
  • Order: merge 1 → 2 → 3 → 4. Don’t turn the writer flag on until the agent’s schema bundle in prod has PRs 2–3, because the agent silently drops markup it doesn’t know.
  • Speed and cost: about 40–75 seconds and roughly $0.15–0.40 per drawing. Detailed subjects cost more.
